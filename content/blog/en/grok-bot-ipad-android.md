**Grok Bot** — SpaceXAI's persistent, tool-using agent — expanded to **iPad and Android** this week and dropped its entry price dramatically. The agent that launched last month at around $300 a month now works with a **$20/month Cursor Pro plan**, and it's free for Cursor enterprise customers during a promotional window.

That combination — more platforms and a much lower floor — is part of a broader pattern in 2026: always-on agents are moving from premium toys to mainstream subscriptions. Here's what changed, why it matters, and what it means for anyone picking a personal agent.

## What changed this week

Grok Bot originally shipped as an iPhone and Mac app with a very expensive price tag. Two things moved this week:

- **New platforms.** Grok Bot is now available on iPad and Android, joining the iPhone and Mac apps that arrived at launch.
- **Cheaper access.** The price to get past the free trial has come down sharply. Grok Bot now works with **Cursor Pro ($20/month)**, which makes it comparable to other starter AI subscriptions instead of a premium product. For Cursor enterprise customers, it's free for the next couple of weeks.

The expansion was reported on September 4, 2026, and follows SpaceXAI's acquisition of Cursor, which has helped it become more competitive after falling behind in the AI space.

## What Grok Bot actually is

Grok Bot is an iMessage-style AI system that displays agents as teammates. Rather than a single chat window, it's designed around several agents that show up as contacts, each with its own role and persistent state. It runs as an always-on agent with its own computer, working inside tools and apps the way a person would — a pattern that connects directly to how OpenAI's and Google's agents are evolving.

Grok Bot is jointly developed by SpaceXAI and Cursor, and it's bundled with paid SuperGrok and Cursor subscription plans.

## The persistent-agent model

The most notable difference between Grok Bot and a chatbot is that it doesn't stop when you stop. It's an always-on agent — it holds a task list, carries context between sessions, and continues working even when you're not at the screen.

That's the same direction every major laboratory is moving. OpenAI is prototyping a [persistent mode](https://wolffi.sh/blog/openai-always-on-agent) for its flagship agent, and Google's Gemini Spark runs 24/7. Grok Bot's shift to more platforms and lower prices signals that persistence isn't just a lab concept — it's becoming a consumer product.

## What the price drop changes

The move to a $20 entry point is the part most people will feel. At $300 a month, an always-on agent was a professional tool. At $20, it's in range for a solo person or small team to actually leave running.

There's a real trade-off at lower price points. Community reports and vendor docs have raised questions about memory isolation and token-consumption behavior — meaning a cheap plan that runs a persistent agent all day can still produce a large token bill if you're not watching usage. When you pilot an always-on agent, it's worth setting [usage caps](https://wolffi.sh/blog/ai-agent-cost) and checking per-agent memory boundaries so the cost stays predictable.

## What it means for you

If you've been on the fence about a standing agent, the practical takeaway is that the price and platform barriers are coming down fast. Grok Bot's expansion is one more sign that always-on agents are headed for everyday devices, not just desktops.

If you prefer to keep your data out of a vendor's cloud, the same capability is available in a local-first setup — and it's the [local vs. cloud decision](https://wolffi.sh/blog/local-first-vs-cloud-ai-assistant) that really determines whether a persistent agent is a convenience or a responsibility.

All of this also means there are more reasons than ever to [set up automations that run while you sleep](https://wolffi.sh/blog/schedule-ai-agent-automations). A persistent agent is only as useful as the boundaries you give it — on paper, Grok Bot is now cheap and everywhere, but the value comes from deciding what it's allowed to do.

## The takeaway

Grok Bot's move to iPad, Android, and a $20 plan is the clearest sign yet that always-on agents are becoming a normal subscription. The capability is now accessible to regular people — which is exactly why the rules you set for your agent matter more than the price you pay for it.
