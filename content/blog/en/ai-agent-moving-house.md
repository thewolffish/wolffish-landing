An AI agent is genuinely useful for a house move because a move is mostly administrative: dozens of small tasks, each with a deadline, spread across utilities, government, insurers, and a dozen services you forgot you had. The agent is good at holding that list, drafting the messages, and chasing the ones you have not heard back on. It should never pick your mover, sign anything, or pay a deposit.

Set that boundary and the move stops eating your evenings.

## The division of labour

A move splits cleanly into three kinds of work: **decide**, **execute**, and **notice**.

- **Decide** is yours. Which mover, which neighbourhood, what to keep, what to pay. An agent can present options, but the moment it chooses for you it is guessing at your priorities.
- **Execute** is shared. Filling forms, sending the same address change to fourteen companies, cancelling internet, booking a cleaner — this is where the time goes, and where an agent earns its keep.
- **Notice** is entirely the agent's. Whether the insurer replied, whether the utility confirmed the transfer date, whether the second quote ever arrived. Human memory is bad at this and agents are excellent at it.

## The move timeline, and who does what

Most moves go wrong in week four, not on moving day — a service that was never cancelled, or one that was never transferred.

| When | Task | Best owner | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 weeks out | Get mover quotes, verify licences | You, agent drafts | A quote is a judgement call; the research is not |
| 6 weeks out | Declutter and inventory | Both | The agent turns photos and room notes into a list |
| 4 weeks out | Notify utilities, council or municipality, bank, insurer | Agent drafts, you approve | High volume, low judgement, easy to forget |
| 2 weeks out | Mail forwarding, address on ID, subscriptions | Agent prompts you | Deadline-sensitive, mostly your signature |
| Moving week | Confirm every service on both ends | Agent | Pure follow-up work |
| Week after | Meter readings, cleaning, old keys, deposits | Both | Depends on paperwork you are holding |

## Change of address: the one part you should do yourself

Mail forwarding is worth doing properly, and it is the task most likely to be sold to you twice. In the US, the official route is [USPS.com/move](https://www.usps.com/umove/) — third-party sites charge far more for exactly the same submission. The same pattern holds everywhere: the government channel is the cheap one, and the polished-looking form in a search ad is the expensive one.

Where an agent helps is the twenty other places that need the new address: bank, insurer, doctor, pharmacy, employer, pension, subscriptions, loyalty schemes, delivery accounts. Give it the list once and it can draft every message in your voice. Do not give it the authority to submit official records in your name — you keep that.

## Getting mover quotes without getting scammed

Movers are one of the few categories where the failure mode is a stranger holding your furniture hostage. The [FTC's advice on mover scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/09/avoid-scams-when-you-hire-moving-company) is short and specific, and every point of it is worth handing to your agent as a screening rule:

- Never sign paperwork with blank spaces for prices, dates, or signatures.
- Never accept a demand for cash or a large deposit before the move.
- For interstate moves in the US, confirm the mover is registered — you can check the licence number directly at [FMCSA Protect Your Move](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/protect-your-move).
- Insist on an in-home or video survey rather than a phone-only estimate.

This is a good example of a task where the agent should do the *reading* and you do the *deciding*. Have it collect each quote into a table — price, deposit, survey method, licence number, cancellation terms — and compare. The moment one company does not want to be in that table, you have your answer.

## Utilities, insurance, and the tail of subscriptions

The after-move admin is quietly the most expensive part. Utilities bill you at the old address if a transfer was never logged, insurance can lapse the day you change address, and subscriptions renew silently at a place you no longer live.

Give the agent a deadline rule rather than a task list: *anything tied to the address gets confirmed two weeks before and one week after.* Then let it hold the thread. This is the same pattern behind [automating bills and utilities](https://wolffi.sh/blog/ai-agent-pay-bills-utilities) — the agent tracks and prompts, you approve.

## What to never hand over

Four things stay with you through the whole move: signing a contract, paying a deposit, choosing a mover, and anything involving identity documents or official records. Everything else is fair game.

The [safety-patterns guide in the docs](https://docs.wolffi.sh/extending/safety-patterns) is the general version of that rule, and it is worth reading before a move because the stakes are concentrated: a move is a short window where a lot of money and personal data move at once. If you are still setting your agent up, [what to automate first](https://wolffi.sh/blog/what-to-automate-first-ai-agent) covers picking a job narrow enough to trust.

## How to start, in one afternoon

1. Write the move date and the new address into a file the agent can read.
2. Paste in the list of every company that knows your current address. Do not curate it — dump it.
3. Ask the agent to sort the list into "needs a message", "needs a form", and "needs my signature", with a date for each.
4. Have it draft every message but send nothing without your approval for the first week.
5. Then, for the low-stakes ones only, let it send and report back.

Steps one to three take about forty minutes and they are the whole move. The rest of the eight weeks is follow-up, which is exactly what an agent does better than you.

## Takeaway

An AI agent turns a house move from a memory test into a tracked list: it handles the address changes, the quote comparisons, and the chasing, while you keep the signature, the deposit, and the choice of mover. Set it up once with the full list of who knows your address, keep official records and money in your own hands, and the most administratively miserable month of the year becomes a weekly review.
