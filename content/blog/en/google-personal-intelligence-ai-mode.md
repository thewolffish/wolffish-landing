Google's Personal Intelligence is an opt-in feature that connects your Gmail, Google Photos, and YouTube history to AI Mode in Search, so the assistant can answer questions using what it knows about your life. It rolled out globally in May 2026 to nearly 200 countries and 98 languages, and it no longer requires a paid subscription.

## What Personal Intelligence actually does

Before Personal Intelligence, AI Mode gave everyone the same answer to the same question. Personal Intelligence changes that: it connects the dots across your own Google apps so a query like "what should I pack for my trip this weekend" can draw on the flight confirmation in your Gmail and the weather at your destination, rather than asking you to retype everything.

[Google's announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) frames it as the difference between knowing the world's information and understanding your context. The apps it can pull from, per [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/865548/google-ai-mode-search-personal-intelligence-update), are Gmail, Google Photos, YouTube, and your Search history.

## How it works under the hood

It's not a separate product — it's a layer of context. When you opt in, Google connects your apps to AI Mode, and the assistant reads from those sources to personalize answers. [TechCrunch's original report](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/googles-ai-mode-can-now-tap-into-your-gmail-and-photos-to-provide-tailored-responses/) describes it as tailoring responses by connecting across your Google ecosystem.

A few things worth knowing about how it's scoped:

- **It's opt-in.** You choose which apps to connect, and you can disconnect any of them at any time.
- **It's personal accounts only.** Personal Intelligence works on personal Google accounts, not Workspace domains.
- **It's app-by-app.** You can grant Gmail access without handing over Photos, and vice versa.

That granularity is the difference between this and an assistant that demands standing, all-or-nothing access up front.

## How to turn it on (and off)

Google doesn't force it on you. To enable it:

1. Open the Gemini app or AI Mode in Search.
2. Look for Personal Intelligence in settings.
3. Connect the apps you want — Gmail, Photos, YouTube, Search history.
4. Ask a question that would benefit from your context (a trip, a planned purchase, or a "what did I do last week" type question).

To turn it off, disconnect the apps the same way. Because it's opt-in and app-by-app, you stay in control of what's in the loop and what isn't.

## The privacy trade-off

The promise is a more useful assistant; the cost is the same as any assistant that reads your mail — you're giving a model visibility into the parts of your life those apps contain. What makes the trade-off feel bigger here is scale: this is Google's default stack, so the assistant already has your search and YouTube behavior, and Personal Intelligence layers your email and photos on top.

That's not automatically bad. It's genuinely useful for someone who wants a proactive assistant without setting anything up. But it's worth reading the same way you'd read [how much access you hand an agent](https://wolffi.sh/blog/ai-agent-permissions-guide): know what's connected, and disconnect anything you don't want in the loop.

If you'd rather keep that personalized context somewhere you control instead of a cloud account, a [local-first agent](https://wolffi.sh/start) holds your memory on your own machine — the same "it knows you" result, with a different answer to who stores the knowledge.

## Personal Intelligence vs a full personal agent

It's worth separating two things that sound alike. Personal Intelligence makes *search* personal — it reads your context so answers fit your life, but it doesn't go off and do tasks for you. A full personal agent (booking a flight, cleaning your inbox, filing expenses) is a different layer: it acts, not just answers.

That distinction is the whole reason both exist. Personal Intelligence is the low-friction version — free, global, nothing to install. A personal agent is the higher-commitment version — more setup, more access, but it finishes work instead of just answering. If all you want is a search engine that remembers you, Personal Intelligence is the easier on-ramp.
