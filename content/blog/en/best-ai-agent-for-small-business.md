The best AI agent for a small business depends on one question: do you want a ready-made assistant you can point at your tools, or a workflow you build yourself? Right now the strongest all-rounders are Lindy for a conversational assistant that connects to your apps, and Zapier for no-code automation — with Microsoft Copilot Studio and Salesforce Agentforce better for businesses already living inside those ecosystems.

## What an AI agent actually does for a small business

For a small business, an AI agent is leverage. It does the repetitive connective work a team of one or two usually does by hand: triaging the inbox, chasing leads, drafting follow-ups, updating the CRM, answering the same customer questions. The difference from a chatbot is that an agent strings steps together — read the new lead, check your calendar, draft a reply, log it in the CRM — instead of waiting for one message at a time.

That distinction is the whole reason agents are interesting to a solo owner: a chatbot answers; an agent *does*. The jobs that pay for themselves fastest are the ones where the agent can complete a full loop without you in the middle.

## How to pick without wasting money

The most common small-business mistake is buying a platform before deciding what to automate. That's backwards — the task picks the tool, not the other way around:

- **Inbox + CRM triage** → a conversational agent like Lindy that reads Gmail and updates your CRM.
- **Connecting apps that don't talk** → Zapier (or n8n) for the plumbing.
- **Already on Microsoft/Teams** → Copilot Studio, so the agent lives where your staff already work.
- **Care about privacy or one-time cost** → a local-first agent you run yourself.

The other mistake is measuring value in features instead of hours. A $30–50/month agent only needs to save you a couple of hours a month to pay for itself — but only if you attach it to a real, repeating task, not a vague "help me with everything."

## A 3-step way to start this week

1. **Pick one repeating task** that eats a fixed chunk of your week — lead follow-up, invoice chasing, or FAQ answers are the usual winners.
2. **Attach the cheapest tool that can do that one task** and run it for two weeks. Don't buy the platform tier yet.
3. **Measure the hours saved**, then either expand that workflow or move to the next task.

The same "start with one task" instinct from [what to automate first](https://wolffi.sh/blog/what-to-automate-first-ai-agent) applies here: a narrow agent that reliably does one job beats a broad one that half-does ten.

## What to watch out for

Two things trip up small businesses more than anything. First, **hallucinations** — an agent that drafts customer-facing copy needs a human to read it before it ships. Second, **data** — if you're feeding customer emails and leads into a cloud agent, know where that data lives and whether it's used for training. Both are the same concerns from [how much access you hand an agent](https://wolffi.sh/blog/ai-agent-permissions-guide), and they matter more when it's your customers' information in the loop.

If you'd rather not rent a subscription at all, a [local-first agent](https://wolffi.sh/start) runs on your own machine and can read your email, files, and calendar the same way — without a per-user fee or your customer data sitting in a vendor's cloud. It's more setup, but for a solo owner who values control, it's the option most roundups leave out.
