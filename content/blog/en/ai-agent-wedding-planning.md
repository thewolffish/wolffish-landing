An AI agent can run most of the *administrative* half of a wedding — vendor research, outreach, quote comparison, budget tracking, guest logistics and the follow-up nobody does — while you keep every creative and emotional decision. The trick is knowing which half is which, because the average US wedding now costs about $34,000 and most of the waste hides in untracked deposits, unfollowed quotes and unanswered emails.

## Start with the number, not the venue

The Knot's [2026 Real Weddings Study](https://www.theknotww.com/press-releases/the-knot-worldwide-unveils-2026-real-weddings-study) put the average 2025 wedding at **$34,000**, with an average of **$292 per guest** — up $8 per guest on 2024 — and an average guest count of 117. Guests are the single biggest cost driver, which makes the guest list the first thing to model rather than the last.

Give your agent the raw inputs: total budget, your two guest-count scenarios, your city, your date window. Then ask for the arithmetic you would otherwise avoid:

- cost per guest at 80, 117 and 150
- what remains for venue, food, photography, attire and music after a 10% buffer
- the deposit schedule that follows from that budget, by month
- the three line items most likely to overrun, based on your city

You are not outsourcing the decision. You are outsourcing the spreadsheet — and getting a version you can argue with.

## Build a vendor pipeline, not a vendor list

This is where an agent earns its keep. Instead of a folder of tabs, ask for a tracked pipeline where every vendor has a state.

| Stage | What the agent does | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | Shortlist 12–15 vendors per category from public listings, with price bands and availability notes | Cut to 4 |
| Outreach | Send tailored enquiries with your date, headcount and budget band | Approve the wording once |
| Quotes | Normalise every reply into one comparable table — inclusions, exclusions, overtime, tax | Mark the two you'd actually book |
| Follow-up | Chase non-responders on day 3 and day 10 | Nothing |
| Booking | Prepare the contract summary and deposit reminder | Sign and pay |

The follow-up row is the one people underestimate. Vendors are busy, and a quote that never arrives looks identical to a vendor that said no. An agent that chases automatically converts dead threads into options.

## Normalise quotes, or you will compare noise

Two photography quotes that look similar are rarely similar. Ask the agent to force every quote into the same columns — hours covered, number of edited images, second shooter, travel, delivery time, deposit, cancellation terms, total before tax — and then rank on total cost at *your* requirements rather than the headline price.

A table built that way regularly shows a "cheaper" vendor costing more once overtime and travel are counted. The point is not that AI finds a hidden discount; it is that the comparison you would have done badly, by hand, at 11pm, gets done properly.

## The guest side is pure logistics

Guest lists, RSVPs, dietary requirements, seating, transport and hotel blocks are all structured-data problems — exactly what an agent handles well.

1. Keep one canonical guest list with a status column per household (invited, RSVP yes, RSVP no, meal, plus-one, travel).
2. Have the agent reconcile replies against it weekly and name the gaps out loud.
3. Ask for seating drafts as constraints, not opinions: family groups together, no ex-partners adjacent, elderly guests near exits and away from speakers.
4. Track the headcount that actually feeds catering, because that number is money.

## Where an agent should stop

Be explicit about this, in writing, in the agent's instructions:

- **It never contacts a guest directly.** Guests hear from you. An agent that emails your aunt is a story you do not want.
- **It never signs, pays or commits.** Deposits and contracts are yours. An agent can prepare and remind; it should not transact without your approval.
- **It never makes taste decisions.** Colours, vows, music, the ceremony itself — those are not optimisation problems.
- **It never sources on vibes alone.** Every recommendation should cite the listing or page it came from, so you can check it.

These are the same boundaries that matter for any financial task; the [audit trail habits](https://wolffi.sh/blog/ai-agent-audit-trail) that make agent bookkeeping trustworthy apply here too.

## A one-month starting sequence

If you are starting now rather than a year out, this order keeps you sane:

- **Week 1:** guest list and budget model. Nothing else.
- **Week 2:** venue and caterer enquiries out; photography shortlist.
- **Week 3:** normalise quotes, book the two that gate everything else.
- **Week 4:** remaining vendors, deposit calendar, and a weekly reconciliation you actually keep.

## The takeaway

Use an agent for the parts of wedding planning that are admin in disguise — budgets, pipelines, quote normalisation, guest logistics, follow-up — and keep the parts that are actually about you. Set the boundaries before you start, especially around contacting guests and spending money, and the same setup will serve you for every large event you plan afterwards.
