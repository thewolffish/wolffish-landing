## The revolution already happened. Nobody sent a notification.

A few years ago we were arguing about whether AI could write a clean paragraph. Now it writes the software, opens the pull request, reviews its own work, and books the flight before you've finished your coffee. Somewhere between "AI" and "agents," the whole thing mutated — and no one issued a press release.

That's how the big shifts always land. Not with a bang. With a slow, quiet *"wait… when did it start handling that?"*

The AI revolution became the **agent revolution** while we were busy debating prompts. So here's where we think it goes next — not in fifty years, in the next handful.

## Prediction 1: The interface is the next thing to die

Look at what an AI harness actually does. It reads the screen, understands the goal, and acts. Every month it needs *you* a little less to get from intent to result.

Follow that line far enough and you reach a strange place: the user interface of the operating system becomes dead weight. Menus, windows, settings panels, the thousand little buttons we've spent forty years learning — they exist because *humans* needed a way to talk to the machine. The moment an agent can talk to the machine directly, that entire layer is just friction wearing a nice coat of paint.

> The UI was never the point. It was the translation layer between you and the computer. Remove the need to translate, and the UI quietly disappears.

We don't think agents will *sit on top of* the OS forever. We think they go deeper, and deeper, until they reach the floor — and then they replace the floor. The interface of the future isn't a better dashboard. It's no dashboard at all. You say what you want. It happens.

## Prediction 2: Software stops being software. It becomes self-evolving.

This is the prediction we're most sure about and least able to prove, so let's name it: **self-evolving software.**

Today we ship a fixed thing. A frozen block of code that runs a task the same way for everyone, forever, until a human pushes an update. That model is about to look ancient.

The next generation of software won't be shipped as a finished product. It'll be shipped as a **factory** — a system whose entire job is to generate its own configs, its own UI, its own backend, *on demand,* shaped to the exact thing you're trying to do in that exact moment. It doesn't have one face. It builds the face the situation needs and throws it away when it's done.

Which means self-evolving software doesn't really *look* like anything. Two people running "the same" product would be running two different things — different screens, different logic, different shape — because the software grew itself around each of them.

> Software stops being a noun you install. It becomes a verb that happens.

We stop shipping *answers.* We start shipping the thing that writes the answer, live, for one person, once.

## Prediction 3: Tokens go to zero

Here's the one we genuinely can't fully explain — but we'd bet on it anyway.

The cost of running these agents — tokens, compute, all of it — trends to zero. Not "cheap." **Zero.** And we think it happens the way roads happened.

Cars were the automotive industry's product. But *roads* — the thing that made cars actually useful — got absorbed by governments and treated as public infrastructure, because moving people and goods turned out to be too important to leave as a billing line item. Nobody pays a meter every time they drive to work. The road is just *there.*

Intelligence is heading the same way. Once an entire civilization runs on agents, the cost of thought becomes too load-bearing to meter. It gets treated as infrastructure — the new roads, the new electricity, the new water — paid for collectively, because a society that has to ask "can I afford to think about this?" can't function. We're not sure of the mechanism. We're fairly sure of the destination.

## Prediction 4: Everyone gets an agent. A real one.

Not a chatbot. Not an app. An agent — singular, yours, lifelong.

It takes over the tedious operation of your life. It tracks what you do, learns how you think, teaches you what you're missing, and — this is the part people underestimate — it keeps you company. It's the assistant, the tutor, the operator, and the presence, all in one continuous thread that knows your whole story.

This is the future Wolffish is building toward. An agent that lives close to you, runs on your terms, and remembers in plain files you can actually read. Not a black box you rent. A mind you own.

## The uncomfortable part

If agents do the operating, the building, and the deciding — what are *we* for?

We don't get to skip this question, so let's sit in it. The honest answer is that humanity is about to spend a generation **repurposing itself.** We'll keep offloading capability to AI — work, logistics, decisions, whole careers — and we'll keep asking what's left that's ours.

And here's the surprising part: this might be the moment **Elon is right this time.** The combination he's been pointing at — universal basic income plus a civilization that turns toward meaning, creativity, and entertainment instead of toil — stops sounding like a slogan and starts sounding like a description. If thought is free and labor is automated, the scarce thing left isn't productivity. It's purpose. And purpose is something you have to *choose,* not optimize.

That's not a dystopia and it's not a utopia. It's just the next shape of the deal.

## Where we stand

We could be wrong on the timeline. We could be wrong on the mechanism. We're pretty confident on the direction: **interfaces dissolve, software starts building itself, intelligence becomes infrastructure, and every person gets a mind beside them.**

Wolffish is a bet on the good version of that future — one where your agent is transparent, local, and *yours,* not a meter running in someone else's data center. The UI is dying. We'd rather help build what comes after it than mourn it.

See you on the other side of the last interface.
